## 2. Critical findings (independent verdicts)

### A. L-SHAPE ORIENTATION
The rear courtyard ("patio" in the RES survey) is L-shaped in plan, but the L is formed by the **building's footprint** rather than by an L-shaped patio per se. Specifically:

- The basement of Flat 1 is **5.70 m** wide and runs ~19.06 m front-to-back.
- The ground floor is only **4.34 m** wide — i.e., the ground floor is set back from the basement in plan, creating an exterior space at ground-floor level that is wider than the building's GF footprint.
- The rear courtyard at LOWER-BASEMENT level is approximately **3.25 m wide × 2.95 m deep** (Grahame's hand measurement), bounded on three sides by walls.

Standing in the courtyard looking AT the building:
- **REAR WALL (in front of you / the wall with the double doors at low level + tall sash at upper level)**: this is the main rear wall of the building. The lower-basement double-door bay is recessed in this wall. The wide multi-pane sash at ground-floor level is in this wall.
- **LEFT WALL (the side wall of the building's main mass, returning forward into the courtyard)**: this is the wall with the basement-kitchen sash window (lower) AND the bricked-up former opening (upper, above and to the left of the kitchen window). The cast-iron downpipe runs vertically along this wall.
  - Equivalently: this is the wall returning from the rear wall along the SHORT side of the L.
- **RIGHT WALL** = boundary wall with the neighbour to the south (No. 18); not a building wall.
- **REAR BOUNDARY WALL** = high (~3.5 m) wall at the very rear of the demise.
- **INSIDE CORNER OF THE L** = the corner where the rear wall meets the left wall = the corner where the spiral staircase will sit (per Grahame's brief and per the AI key-plan).

**My independent verdict on orientation:** the bricked-up opening is on the **LEFT WALL** (the wall returning forward from the rear wall along the side of the projecting building wing). The staircase goes in the **inside corner of the L** (which is the corner where the LEFT wall meets the REAR wall). The new doorway formed by reinstating + extending downward the bricked-up opening will sit at the TOP of the staircase, on the LEFT WALL. This is consistent with the ChatGPT key-plan in attempt 3.

**Compass orientation:** the inset map on job16873-1.pdf shows north pointing UP-slightly-right; No. 22 is to the north of No. 20 and No. 18 is to the south. The building's front faces ~WEST (onto Hornton Street). Therefore the REAR of No. 20 faces ~EAST. The LEFT wall of the courtyard (as I defined "left" above, standing in the courtyard looking at the building) is the wall on the NORTH side of the courtyard — i.e., the wall adjacent to No. 22's boundary. The rear wall is on the EAST side. **So: REAR wall = east; LEFT wall (with bricked-up opening) = north (adjacent to No. 22).**

### B. DOOR-BAY SYMMETRY (lower-basement double doors in their recess)
Examining photo `shared image (14).jpg` at high zoom, the painted-white recessed door bay in the rear wall has approximately equal cheeks left and right. The doors + flanking sidelights are centred within the recess; the red-brick lintel above is centred over the recess. There is mild apparent asymmetry in the photo but I judge this to be perspective distortion (the photo is taken from upper-left looking down-right, which foreshortens the right side of the bay).
