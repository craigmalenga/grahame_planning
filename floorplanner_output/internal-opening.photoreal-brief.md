# Photoreal Brief — Internal Opening, Front Lounge ↔ Rear Reception — AFTER (proposed)

## SCENE
Interior view, late-Victorian Grade-II listed Kensington terraced flat. Standing in the front lounge looking through the newly-enlarged opening into the rear reception. Tall ceilings, period cornicing and moulded skirtings, wide-plank polished oak floor. Daylight from the rear-reception sash beyond, throwing light across the floor toward the viewer.

## LOCKED DIMENSIONS (do not invent or change)
- Both rooms ceiling: **3.28 m**
- Front lounge internal width: **2.65 m**
- Rear reception internal width: **3.00 m** (+350 mm wider than front)
- Internal dividing wall thickness: **100 mm**
- External walls: ~300 mm thick
- **Proposed opening:** clear width **1350 mm × 2950 mm head height**
- Retained nibs: **L = 700 mm, R = 600 mm** (measured from each lounge-side corner)
- Steel beam over the opening: ~250 mm deep zone (concealed in plaster downstand)
- Rear-reception external sash (visible through the opening): **2100 × 2200 mm**, sill 300 mm above floor

## MATERIALS
- Walls: pale cream painted plaster
- Floor: wide-plank polished oak, mid-tone
- Cornice + skirting + architraves: white painted softwood with period mouldings
- Doors / sash frames: white painted timber
- Steel beam above opening: NOT visible (concealed inside the plaster downstand that runs across the head). Visible cornice profile is retained and re-run across the new head
- No furniture in shot (or minimal: one wing-back armchair acceptable for scale)

## LIGHTING
Mid-morning daylight, soft and diffuse from the rear-reception window behind the opening. Subtle warm fill from a single pendant light visible in each room. No harsh direct sun.

## COMPOSITION
Camera at ~1.6 m eye-level, centred in the front lounge ~3 m back from the opening, looking through the opening squarely. The new opening fills the centre of the frame; retained nibs visible on each side (L = 700 mm, R = 600 mm = visibly asymmetric); the rear-reception sash window glows in the background. Period cornice runs continuously across the head of the opening.

## STYLE
Architectural / interiors photography, 35 mm equivalent focal length, f/5.6, ISO 400. RAW realism. Calm composition. No CGI artefacts. Sympathetic to Grade-II listed Victorian aesthetic.

## REFERENCE
Match the proportional sense and period detailing of a typical RBKC Kensington listed-property interior (Savills / Knight Frank quality of internal shot). Do NOT invent fireplaces or features unless requested.

## ANTI-PATTERNS — do NOT produce
- Equal nibs left + right — they are deliberately asymmetric (700 / 600 mm)
- Exposed steel beam — the beam is concealed; only the plaster downstand reads
- Modern frameless wide-frame opening — head detail is **plastered with re-run cornice** above
- Lost cornice — period mouldings must continue across the new opening head

## RECOMMENDED ITERATION VIEWS
1. **Hero** — view above (front lounge → through to rear reception).
2. **Reverse** — from rear reception looking back at the front lounge through the opening.
3. **Detail of head + nibs** — close 50 mm focal length crop of the lintel zone showing plaster downstand + retained cornice + nibs with architrave returns.
