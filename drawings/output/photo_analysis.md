# Photo Analysis — Flat 1, 20 Hornton Street

Purpose: establish the *actual* physical geometry of the rear courtyard / lightwell
and the rear elevation of Flat 1, 20 Hornton Street, from the photos that
Grahame (the owner) supplied, and to record explicitly where the four previous
ChatGPT attempts diverged from reality so the next iteration does not repeat
them.

All measurements quoted are Grahame's site-measured figures from his email
(see `read_me.,md`); the photos are used to confirm geometry, orientation and
character, not to scale dimensions off pixels.

---

## Definitive layout (the headline)

The rear courtyard / lightwell is an L-shaped cut-out. Standing *in* the
courtyard and looking back AT the building (i.e. facing the building from
outside):

- **LEFT-hand wall of the L** = a side wall of the courtyard. It runs
  forward from the rear wall on the LEFT. This is the wall that, at
  *upper* (ground-floor) level, has the **bricked-up former opening** that
  is to be reinstated and converted into a doorway to receive the top of
  the spiral staircase. At *lower* (basement) level the same left-hand
  wall has an existing sash window.
- **RIGHT-hand wall of the L** = the rear wall proper. At *lower*
  (basement) level this wall has the **double doors** in a recessed
  opening, centred between equal stubs of white-painted brickwork. At
  *upper* (ground-floor) level the same rear wall has the **large
  multi-pane sash window** (the existing rear reception-room window).
- **The inside corner of the L** (where the left wall meets the rear
  wall) is where the **reclaimed cast-iron spiral staircase** sits. The
  staircase tucks into the corner so that its top tread / landing
  presents at the new doorway formed in the bricked-up opening on the
  left wall, and its base lands on the courtyard slab clear of the
  lower-basement double doors on the rear wall.

This is the mirror of one of the configurations Grahame tested in
correspondence — he was clear (in his message) that the L runs
"left-wall meets rear-wall", not the other way round.

respect. Does not constrain the rear drawing geometrically except to fix
the *style language* (multi-pane white-painted sashes, black cast iron,
red brick + stone bands).

### shared image (13).jpg — the LEFT-hand wall of the L, lower courtyard
Phone shot looking sideways at the LEFT-hand wall of the L from inside the
courtyard. Shows: (a) a white-painted brick lower section to roughly
window-head height, with a multi-pane sash window — this is the *lower*
(basement-level) window on the left-hand wall; (b) above the white line,
yellow-stock brickwork unpainted; (c) a black-painted cast-iron

There is **no upper window visible on this wall** — the upper opening has
been bricked-in, flush with the rest of the brickwork. This is the
opening to be reinstated as a doorway. The white painted line on the
brick gives a clear datum: it sits roughly at the top of the
lower-window head / just above. Floor of the courtyard is reddish
loose material (probably brick dust / gravel) over a slab.

Constraint for the drawing: left-wall lower window must be drawn; left-wall
upper window is **absent** in the existing elevation (just brick) and
must be drawn as a marked dashed "blocked-up opening to be reinstated".
The downpipe runs on this left-hand wall and must be shown / rationalised.

### shared image (14).jpg — looking down into the courtyard, rear wall
Top-down / slightly oblique view down into the courtyard, with the rear
wall (RIGHT-hand wall of the L) facing camera. The double doors sit in a
recessed bay (a slight setback in the wall), framed by stubby
white-painted brick piers / cheeks of **visibly equal width** on the
left and right of the doors. There is a brick-arch lintel over the
doors in red brick (contrasting with the yellow stock to either side and
above). The doors themselves are a pair of part-glazed timber doors with
small flanking fixed lights at sill height. Above the doors / arch the
wall returns to yellow brick and continues up to the large rear sash
window (visible at top of the frame as the white sash heads).

The plant pot and the foliage on the right side reduce visibility of the
right-hand boundary wall, but the *symmetry of the door bay* is the key
point and it is **unambiguous**: door is centred, equal cheek either
side. This directly contradicts the ChatGPT attempts (see below).

Useful measurements (from Grahame): overall rear-opening recess width
185 cm; doors flanked by 72 cm of cheek each side; sill height 80 cm;
height to the white painted line above 290 cm. The brick arch above the
doors is visible.

## ChatGPT attempt notes — what was wrong

### chatgpt attempt 1.png (and the duplicate ChatGPT Image May 24, 2026, 06_09_48 PM.png)
Photo-realistic montage with "existing" and "proposed" rear elevations,
a section through the lightwell, an external materials list and a key
plan. The proposed elevation shows a spiral staircase against what is
drawn as a **flat rear wall** (no L). The key plan, however, *does*
show an L-shape — and locates the staircase as a triangle in the inside
corner of the L. **Wrong because**: the rear elevation drawing has been
flattened, losing the corner; the staircase as drawn appears to spiral
in front of the rear wall rather than nestling into the corner; the
double-door composition is also drawn with **wide left-hand cheek and
narrow right-hand cheek**, not the equal cheeks that the photo shows.

### chatgpt attempt 2.png
Iteration of the same montage. The "proposed rear elevation" panel now
shows the spiral staircase but still presents the rear wall as **flat
(no L)**. The "internal elevation — open door positions" panel below
suggests an internal-door elevation has been bolted on, but the
geometry of the courtyard is still wrong: no corner is shown and the
asymmetry of the door bay is still inverted (wide left).

### chatgpt attempt 3.png
First attempt at a 3D massing. **Getting closer**: the L-shape now
appears — there is a return wall on the left of the proposed view, and
the staircase sits in the corner. However, the L is the **wrong
handedness** in the existing view (the model shows the rear wall
turning away to the LEFT and the lower window on what should be the
left side actually drawn on the right), and the proposed view shows the
**upper left opening as an existing window** that is then lowered —
whereas in reality the upper-left opening is **bricked-up brick wall**
in the existing condition. The brief was clear: it is a reinstatement
of a former opening, not a modification of an existing window.

### chatgpt attempt 4.png
Composite sheet with proposed elevations, internal door elevations, a
plan, and a spiral-staircase enlargement. The plan view is the most
useful element but still shows the staircase too far from the corner
(approximately mid-wall) and the double-door composition is still drawn
with **unequal cheeks** (wide on the left, narrow on the right). The
bathroom/utility layout overlaid in this sheet is a separate question
and is unrelated to the rear-elevation errors.

## Cross-cutting errors to NOT repeat

1. **L-shape handedness**: left-hand wall of the L (looking at building)
   carries the bricked-up upper / sash lower; right-hand wall of the L
   (the rear wall proper) carries the double doors lower / large sash
   upper. Several ChatGPT attempts mirrored this.
2. **The rear wall is recessed, not flat**: there is a corner. Do not
   draw the rear elevation as a single planar wall.
3. **Door bay symmetry**: the double doors are centred in their recess
   with **equal cheeks (72 cm each side)**. Do not draw a wide-left /
   narrow-right composition.
4. **Upper-left opening is bricked-up, not a window**: in the existing
   elevation it must read as blank brickwork with a faint dashed outline
   showing the former opening. The proposal then opens it up as a
   doorway.
5. **Staircase position**: in the inside corner of the L, not
   mid-wall, not in front of the rear wall.
6. **Pipework**: the black cast-iron downpipe on the LEFT wall currently
   runs through the line of the bricked-up opening; the proposal must
   re-route it to clear the new doorway / staircase landing.
7. **Sill datum**: existing rear-window sills sit ~380 cm above
   courtyard slab; existing kitchen-window sill (the one being
   converted to a door on the left-hand wall, upper level) sits at
   ~372 cm. Small but real ~8 cm step in datums. Note on the drawing.
8. **Sash style**: white-painted timber sashes with glazing bars, on a
   yellow-stock brick rear with red-brick flat arches over openings,
   white-paint to courtyard-level brickwork up to a consistent datum
   line, black painted cast-iron rainwater goods. Materials palette
   must match.

## Constraints summary for the next drawing iteration

- Courtyard internal dimensions: ~325 cm wide x ~295 cm deep (Grahame).
- L-shape: left wall and rear wall meet at inside corner where staircase
  is to sit.
- Staircase envelope: ~170-175 cm circular allowance recommended; fits
  in the courtyard with clearance to be confirmed on plan.
- Rear wall (right of L) lower: double doors 144 cm clear + cheeks
  72 cm each = 288 cm wide recess (Grahame's 185 cm overall recess
  width measurement appears to be of the *recess opening itself*, not
  including the painted cheeks — needs re-check on site).
- Rear wall (right of L) upper: existing large sash, 123 cm wide.
- Left wall of L upper: bricked-up opening 60 cm wide (Grahame),
  to be reinstated as a doorway (so will need to be widened
  vertically and possibly horizontally to take a 70-80 cm door).
- Left wall of L lower: existing sash (basement kitchen window).
- White paint datum: 290 cm above lower-basement courtyard floor.
- Sill heights (existing rear-wall sashes): 90 cm under-sill plus
  160 cm opening height.
- Floor-to-floor (basement to ground): ~282 cm based on the previous
  ChatGPT section (FFL -2.82 to FFL 0.00); staircase rise ~3.0-3.15 m
  including landing step-up.
